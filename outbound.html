outbound

masterworks, august 2025 – december 2025. client services associate. a floor with targets.

I ran outbound. Calls and email. I prospected, qualified, and onboarded 200+ accounts. Portfolios booked in HubSpot over $80 million. I found the decision-maker, the timeline, the objection, and the next step, then I handed a clean record to a senior rep who could actually close. That is the job. It is not glamorous. It is a volume sport with a memory.

People who have not done it think outbound is talking. The talking is the easy part if you can talk. The job is a system: list, attempt, note, follow-up, hygiene, coaching, revise the talk track, do it again before lunch. If the CRM is a graveyard, the team is flying blind. I kept HubSpot true because a lying pipeline is worse than a small one.

The product was complicated — art as an alternative asset, a regulated conversation. You cannot fake fluency and survive a real one. I had to know enough to be accurate, and humble enough to get a specialist when accuracy required one. That is the same muscle as not telling a client the irrigation is "fine" when you have not opened the box.

I came from jobs where you see the person. On a roof, the owner is standing there. On a dialer, they are a name until they are not. The hard-nosed part is not being rude. The hard-nosed part is not flinching from the number of no's, and not letting a no become a story you tell yourself so you can stop. Two hundred-plus calls a day, 45–50 hour weeks when the floor asked for it. You either like the pace or you leave. I liked the pace.

What I want a hiring manager to take from this, especially if you are hiring for a serious product: I will touch the unglamorous loop. I will not wait to be inspired. I will keep the system honest. I can hold something complicated in my head without turning it into mush. And I already know what it feels like to represent a thing people have feelings about, and to still have to hit the number.

Masterworks was short. It was also the first time I ran that loop at software-company density — metrics, coaching, a floor. The gardens taught me the system. This taught me the grind. I want the next one to be a product I would actually use.
